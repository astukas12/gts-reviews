Key takeaways

  • Blaney was the long-run car. Ryan Blaney, Chase Briscoe, Christopher Bell were first through third over 10 laps; Blaney also led the 30-lap window (Tyler Reddick next).
  • Fastest single lap: Chase Briscoe (32.680), then Austin Cindric and Denny Hamlin.
  • Longest run: Ty Gibbs, 41 laps. Kyle Larson, the most expensive driver, never ran more than 16 laps in a row, so his long-run rank (11) sits far below his price rank (1).
  • Running better than their price: AJ Allmendinger, Alex Bowman (long-run rank vs price rank 9 vs 23, 10 vs 18).
  • Worse than their price, or no long run: Kyle Larson, William Byron, Chase Elliott, Bubba Wallace, Josh Berry, Ryan Preece.
  • 37 of 40 cars got a 10-lap run and 6 got a 30-lap run, so the 30-lap column is thin.

The board

How to read it. Each cell is the rank, then the gap to the best car in seconds per lap. A run is consecutive clean green laps (out-lap, in-lap and any lap 12% off the car's best are dropped). Best lap is the fastest lap from a run of 3+ laps. Long is the best 10-lap and best 30-lap average inside runs of 10+ laps. Short (runs of 3-9 laps, best 5-lap average) is small because it has added little. Runs of 1-2 laps are in no rank column; they show in blue in the last column. Price means the DraftKings salary, so the ranks show who practised faster or slower than their price suggests. Times are raw lap times.

About the data

All numbers come from the complete lap-by-lap record of Friday's Cup practice. Times are raw: a car on fresher tyres gets a small edge (see the tyre-age chart), and practice runs are not race conditions or fuel loads. Short and long are measured only from runs the car actually made, so a dash means no run of that length. Practice is one input among many; it does not predict wrecks, pit strategy or restarts.
