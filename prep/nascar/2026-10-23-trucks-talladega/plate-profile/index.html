Talladega Trucks: plate profile (DRAFT)

Love's RV Stop 225, race id 5689, 85 laps, 23 Oct 2026. Local draft, not published. Built from NascarData.xlsx, all plate races (Daytona + Talladega) 2022 to 2026: 9 Trucks, 18 O'Reilly, 19 Cup. Starting grid not set (qualifying 12:30 ET), so every start position is assumed average.

What the data says

1. The wreck is close to a coin flip for every truck. Trucks DNF 25.7% of the time at Daytona and Talladega (races range 10.8% to 41.7%, median 25%). A truck's own DNF history, its team's DNF history and its starting spot do not predict who wrecks: across 1,758 plate driver-races the model barely separates cars (predicted DNF 23% to 29%). Quality has little to do with surviving, so DNF risk moves every truck down about the same amount. Cars that DNF finish around 30th or worse, and none has ever finished top 10.
2. After the wrecks, finish order is only weakly about quality, and plate history helps most. Predicting the order of survivors: plate-race history (coefficient 0.47, t=3.1) and general truck form (0.33, t=2.9) matter. The team factor adds nothing once those are in (0.05, t=0.3). Within-race rank correlation between the prediction and survivor finish is about 0.25, so even the best-rated truck is only a modest favorite. Your read on quality teams is partly supported: TRICON, McAnally Hilgemann, ThorSport and Front Row rate at the top, but through driver form and plate history rather than through the team label.
3. Start position barely matters in Trucks. Survivors starting 1st vs last differ by 0.04 to 0.11 of the field in finish percentile in the Truck-only fit (not significant); pooled across all three series it is 0.12 (start coefficient t=3.9). The model keeps it at the pooled value. Once the grid is set, a deep start costs a few percent at most.
4. Teammates finish together. 43% of Truck top-10 finishers had a teammate also top 10 (36% by chance). In this field the big teams (TRICON 5, McAnally 4, Kaulig 4, ThorSport 4) already produce 46% in the simulation without any extra team link, so no additional bump was added.

How the ladder is built

Each truck gets (a) a DNF probability around 26% (range 19.6% to 27.6%) scaled by a race-level attrition draw taken from the nine Truck plate races, then (b) a survivor-finish score from plate history, general truck form, team form and start position. In each of 12,000 simulated races, wrecked trucks go to the bottom and survivors are ordered by score plus noise (residual sd 0.29 of the field). The ladder is the share of simulations in which each truck finishes at or better than each spot. Two of the 38 entries will not start; values below are conditional on starting, drawn for the 8 weakest-rated entries.

Caveats

  • Small samples: 9 Truck plate races, 327 Truck driver-races. Most of the signal comes from pooling Cup and O'Reilly.
  • Debut and low-history drivers (Annunziata, Butcher, Jones, McFarland, White, Stewart) sit at the field average on form and plate history.
  • The 2022 O'Reilly Talladega race (Sparks 300) shows no DNFs in the database, and the 2025 Cup Talladega race has no status; DNF there uses lap completion.
  • Not modelled: pit strategy, fuel, big-one timing within the race, qualifying draft partners.
