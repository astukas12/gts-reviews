Before practice: what Phoenix practice has told us, and how little four races can settle

Before practice: what has practice told us at Phoenix, using the same readings we will use today. This page is the baseline; when the session is locked the practice report replaces it at this address.

How much to trust this. Only 4 Phoenix O’Reilly races have lap-by-lap practice (Nov 2024, Mar 2025, Nov 2025, Mar 2026). That is thin: one race’s score for any reading swings by about ±0.15 from race to race across the series, so the Phoenix average of four has an error of about ±0.08, as large as most of the gaps between readings. Every track plays differently, so Phoenix is the first place we look, but we do not take its four-race averages as the answer. The 40-race O’Reilly sample beside it is the check: where Phoenix and the series agree we lean on it; where they differ, treat the difference as probably noise.

Read: the long run (best 5- and 10-lap averages on one run) is the reading to watch for how a car runs all day: 0.67 at Phoenix against 0.67 for the series. The single fastest lap scores 0.70 at Phoenix and 0.65 in the series, and the composite of the three scored 0.72 at Phoenix. Pre-race form matched or beat every practice reading in all four Phoenix races (0.86 on average). Practice is a supplement to form, not a replacement. The most recent Phoenix race (Mar 2026) is the weakest for practice: practice ordered it poorly (composite 0.51) while form still ordered it well. We do not know why; one race like that is what four races look like, and it could be noise.

What practice adds on top of form

All 40 O’Reilly races: 0%: 0.827 · 10%: 0.833 · 20%: 0.838 · 30%: 0.834 · 40%: 0.828.

The same vs finishing position — Phoenix: 0%: 0.650 · 10%: 0.646 · 20%: 0.652 · 30%: 0.643 · 40%: 0.641. Series: 0%: 0.618 · 10%: 0.620 · 20%: 0.617 · 30%: 0.612 · 40%: 0.605.

Read: against how a car ran all day, a modest practice weight (20–30%) helps a little in both samples and a large one hurts; against the finish it adds almost nothing at either. With four races we cannot say Phoenix practice is worth more or less than anywhere else. The working range we will use is 10–30% of the ordering for how a car runs all day and 0–10% for the finish, which wrecks and strategy scramble.

Did practice predict the dominators?

In 40 O’Reilly races, the driver who earned the most dominator points practiced in the top 3 in 12% and the top 5 in 32% (a random car would be 8% and 14%). Pre-race form did better: top 3 in 60% and top 5 in 70%; the grid had him top 3 in 50%. Rank correlation with the share of dominator points: form 0.66, practice 0.52, grid 0.61, 10-lap run 0.44. Read: practice is a modest flag for who will dominate and not an extra edge once form and the grid are known (-0.04). It is most useful as a check that a form favorite has the speed.
